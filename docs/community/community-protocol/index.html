#Community Protocol (Work in Progress)

Work in progress. This protocol is itself a Proposal: a starting point for a community, not a finished rulebook. Several parts are deliberately left open (see Open questions), and the rest is open for discussion. Join the protocol thread.

TypeRoof aspires to be foundational type tooling infrastructure, and our strongest asset is our community. This section outlines the principles that guide how we collaborate and gather.

#Three Pillars

This protocol rests on three pillars to create a warm, welcoming, and inclusive space and software we can all stand behind. You can also read the thinking behind this protocol.

#1. Contributor Experience

We want you to learn, explore different roles, and feel proud of what you bring to the table. Whether you are sharing a domain idea or experiment in the Inception phase, writing mentored code in the Incubation phase, or launching and maintaining a custom distribution, you are free to contribute at your own pace and shift roles whenever it suits you. We are also glad to have you if you are just along for the ride in our community forum.

#2. Trust

We elevate the value of contributions and the ecosystem by backing raw ideas and AI-assisted prototypes with honest human curation and architectural mentoring. Trust in our software is earned by putting in the work, communicating transparently, and adhering to the public quality standards of each distribution. Trust between people is earned the same way: in Inception, by giving every Proposal an honest hearing and keeping its trail visible when it grows into a feature; in Incubation, where Builders and Maintainers come to understand the code they ship, and each other; and in Distribution, where Maintainers earn the trust of their users through steady curation and care—while users always remain free to choose another distribution.

#3. FLOSS

We ensure permanent architectural freedom with no black boxes and no vendor lock-in. Everyone has the four freedoms to run, study, modify, and redistribute TypeRoof on their own terms. The core is licensed under the permissive Apache 2.0 license: in an era where AI makes code abundant and easy to replicate, code itself may no longer be the scarce asset worth guarding, so we choose to keep it frictionless to adopt and build on. Distributions may choose their own license—and ideally, improvements still flow back to the core. What we protect instead is what cannot be cloned: trust, human connection, and mutual growth.

#Who is Behind This

TypeRoof is developed and stewarded by Strong Type Systems GmbH, founded by Lasse Fister in Germany. The company maintains the shared core—the tech stack that distributions build on and exchange features through—and has applied to register TypeRoof as a trademark (EUIPO, registration pending).

We state this openly because the protocol asks you to trust people, and that starts with knowing who is accountable. The company’s role is to keep the core stable, interoperable, and freely available under Apache 2.0, and to run the reference distributions. It does not control other distributions, and it cannot take back what has been released: the four freedoms guarantee that TypeRoof outlives any single steward, including us. Contributions to the core are accepted under a Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO), not a contributor license agreement: contributors keep their copyright and everything released under Apache 2.0 stays available under Apache 2.0—no one, including us, can take that back.

The trademark application exists to protect users, not to fence in the code. Once registered, it ensures that “TypeRoof” means what users expect it to mean. Any distribution may describe itself factually as “built on TypeRoof” or “for TypeRoof”. Beyond that, the naming policy is still being worked out; until it is published, please ask before using the TypeRoof name as part of a distribution’s own name.

Strong Type Systems sustains this work through consulting, commissioned feature development, paid Incubation (where a client’s own developer builds a feature as a mentored Builder), and sponsorship. Commissioned features meet the same standards as any other contribution to the core or distribution.

#Three-Phase Contribution Flow

We aim to be open to any kind of contribution and participation, and from there create a path to develop both reliable, trustworthy software and self-determined community actors.

#Inception

An open sandbox where Catalysts—users, domain experts, developers, and vibe-coders—can share Proposals ranging from raw ideas, insights, and sketches to quick hacks and rough prototypes. Inception lowers the pressure of contributing and celebrates the human spark as our primary unit of value. This is an exciting and creative playground to hang out in!

Each Proposal lives in a dedicated forum thread where the community can discuss, clarify, and flesh it out into a candidate for Incubation. Not every Proposal will be picked up or accepted by a distribution—and that is completely normal, never a personal rejection.

Not everything needs to go through Inception: bug fixes and small improvements go directly to a distribution.

#Incubation

Incubation is where a Proposal is mentored into production-ready features for a specific distribution. This phase is pull-based: each distribution chooses which Proposals it would like to incubate. Because each distribution defines its own standards, this phase begins either when a distribution’s Maintainers select a promising Proposal from Inception or when they issue a “Call for Incubation” to attract a Builder for a needed feature.

The Maintainers and the Builder jointly define the success criteria and work through mentored reviews across one or more PR cycles. Afterward, other distributions are free to pick up the finished feature as-is or run their own incubation phase to adapt and refine it to their own criteria.

#Distribution

Multiple autonomous distributions can operate side by side on their own terms, optimizing their collection of features, quality standards, and presentation for distinct target profiles. This gives Maintainers full freedom and system-level mastery while offering users specialized flavors of TypeRoof. Distributions that keep FLOSS licenses compatible with Apache 2.0 and the shared TypeRoof tech stack can seamlessly interoperate and exchange features.

Each distribution is free to define its own internal structure, release criteria, and workflows—including traditional feature-branch and pull-request workflows for direct contributions. Within this protocol, anyone speaking and acting on behalf of a distribution is considered a Maintainer.

#Roles

Contributors can move fluidly between roles depending on how they want to participate at any given moment:

#Catalysts (Inception)

Catalysts drive ideation, experimentation, and rapid prototyping in the Inception phase. They may be domain experts—type designers, typographers, script and language experts—outlining domain logic, users articulating needs, or coders and vibe-coders sharing rough proofs-of-concept. This role values openness, curiosity, and creative momentum: it allows contributors to share boundary-pushing Proposals without taking on the commitment of long-term maintenance or strict architectural compliance.

#Builders (Incubation)

Builders bridge the gap between a raw Proposal and production-ready software during Incubation. Teaming up with a distribution or answering a “Call for Incubation”, they collaborate with Maintainers to engineer, refine, and document a feature until it meets the distribution’s standards. This role enacts craftsmanship and follow-through—delivering solid, dependable features without requiring the contributor to take on the ongoing governance of a distribution.

#Maintainers (Distribution)

Maintainers steward trust, architecture, and stability within a Distribution. They define their distribution’s standards, mentor Builders during Incubation, review contributions, and care for releases and production environments. This role calls for diligence, architectural care, and fair curation—serving as the anchor of reliability across the ecosystem.

#Open Questions

These are the parts we have deliberately left open. Each is an invitation to contribute—bring a Proposal to the forum. For now, Strong Type Systems stewards this document and merges changes to it.

  • Conduct and moderation. How we treat each other, and what happens when that fails. Until defined, the forum’s house rules apply.
  • Incubation lifecycle. What happens when an incubation stalls, a Builder steps away, or Builder and Maintainers disagree on success criteria.
  • AI-assisted contributions. Disclosure, provenance, and sign-off. Distributions and individuals decide for themselves; the core will publish its own policy as a reference. The core should add a supplementary sign-off alongside the DCO, along these lines: “If you used AI tools, you certify that you have reviewed and understand the contribution and that, to your knowledge, it does not incorporate code under an incompatible license.”
  • Upstream flow from copyleft distributions. How features developed under a copyleft license find their way back to the Apache 2.0 core.
